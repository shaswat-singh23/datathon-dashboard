# Top 3 demand changes: Back Forty walkway closure, event lift, June softening
- Issue: #16 | Author: Kyle | Time: 12:45
- Claim: (1) Back Forty demand fell about 30% while the Main Entrance to Back Forty walkway was closed for renovation (Mar 3 to Apr 6 gaming dates), then mostly came back. (2) Sports and property events lift floor coin-in 16 to 23% over the same weekday; conventions add roughly 1 to 6%. (3) June is the softest month, about 3% below the weekday-adjusted norm on non-event days.
- Evidence:
  1. **Walkway closure (location, not machines).** `floor_walkways` W-ENT-B40 closed 2025-03-03 14:00 UTC to 2025-04-07 13:00 UTC. Back Forty (Z-B40), 5 weeks before / 5 closed weeks / 5 weeks after:
     coin-in per available machine-hour **$135.5 / $96.3 / $125.9** (−29% while closed); zone entries per day **603 / 421 / 552** (−30%); occupancy **28.3% / 20.7% / 25.8%**; coin-in per occupied hour **$479 / $464 / $487** (about flat). Rest of floor: $206.1 / $210.7 / $197.4 (no matching drop). Back Forty share of floor coin-in: 11.0% / 7.7% / 10.3%.
     Fewer guests reached the zone. Those who sat down bet about the same.
  2. **Events, weekday-matched.** Lift = event-day coin-in / mean coin-in of non-event days with the same weekday − 1:
     Super Bowl LIX weekend **+23%** (3 days), college basketball opening rounds **+19%** (4), Gilded Ace Winter Slot Tournament **+18%** (3), EDC weekend **+16%** (3), NAB Show +5.6% (5), Locals Appreciation Week +4.1% (7), CinemaCon +2.7% (4), CES +1.4% (4).
     For context: Saturday averages $1.75M/day vs Tuesday $1.09M on non-event days (+60%), so a raw event-vs-non-event comparison would overstate weekend events.
  3. **June softening.** Weekday-adjusted index on non-event days (actual / same-weekday expectation): Jan 0.997, Feb 0.997, Mar 1.012, Apr 1.028, May 0.997, **Jun 0.968**. June coin-in per day is $1.32M, the lowest month (Feb $1.41M peak). Coin-in per available machine-hour: Jun $179.7 vs Feb $197.1. Part of that drop comes from more available hours in Center Pit (Apr to Jun), not only from less play.
  Source: `marts/q2_demand.parquet` (built by `royale/marts/q2_demand.py`), rows grouped by window/zone/weekday in pandas. These are the numbers shown on `pages/2_demand.py` with the zone drill set to Back Forty and the period set to each window. The mart's daily floor totals reconcile exactly with `property_day` (coin-in max diff < 1e-9; entries and available hours exact).
- Alternative explanations: (1) A Back Forty machine mix or fault change in March could also lower demand. Coin-in per occupied hour stays flat and the drop starts and ends on the closure dates, which fits traffic better. The renovation itself may also have made the zone less attractive. (2) Event days are few (3 to 7), and several events overlap weekends and paydays. (3) June could reflect citywide seasonality (not tested here; LVCVA data cannot feed a forecast but may serve as context in #34).
- Limitations: Zone entries are sensor crossings, not unique people. Offline counter hours are missing, not zero (Back Forty coverage was 23 to 24 h on almost every day in the window). No significance test on event lifts.
- So what: Back Forty machines were penalized by a five-week access closure. Any bottom-10 machine sitting in Back Forty may look bad because of **location**, not because of the machine. The Q1 adjusted ranking (#22) should control for zone and the closure window before calling a machine underperforming.
