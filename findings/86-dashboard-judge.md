**Event defect and fix.** `pages/2_demand.py` previously mapped each event day to the selected period's non-event weekday mean, then summed expected values while pandas silently dropped unmatched NaNs. Actual totals still included unmatched days. `royale/demand.py:event_comparison` now counts matched/total event days, preserves full event actuals and suppresses lift plus expected totals unless every day is matched. Zero expected totals also suppress lift. The page tells judges to widen the period and distinguishes descriptive comparisons from causal effects. Jan 1–7 is 0/1 matched; Jan 1–10 is 3/4; all January is 4/4 with finite CES lift approximately +1.9156%. Unit fixtures also distinguish ratio-of-sums lift from averaging daily lifts. Zone drilling uses the same selected population for actuals and weekday baselines.

**Utility improvement.** The Model page retains constant/weekday/fitted forecast MAEs and the fixed Jan–Apr/May–Jun split. It adds a concrete operational-review scenario with 1/2/3 equal-length review days per complete week. At two slots, Friday/Saturday covers 35.6841% of held-out wagers versus uniform expected 28.5714%, gain 7.1126 percentage points, exploratory week-bootstrap interval [6.4573, 7.8579]. The interval does not establish a July or service benefit. Full trace, competing explanation and limitations: `findings/86-demand-planning.md`, accessible in the Model Source document picker without GitHub access.

**Preserved strengths.** Home already displays the bounded replacement decision, January–June inspection plan, daily/bank controls, same-population metrics, Source and month-removal rank sensitivity. All eight app routes expose committed evidence inside the app, avoiding private-repository access requirements. Money, off-floor exclusion, dated placements, DST exposure, time-based validation and honest losing-regression comparison were already sound. The priced inspection explicitly buys information without estimated positive recovery. Neither losing to a simple forecast baseline nor preserving most budget warrants a deduction under the published rules.

**Traffic exposure.** Review identified 211/1,267 zone-days with sensor coverage below scheduled hours (coverage ranges 3–24 hours). Demand now retains counts explicitly labeled observed, adds observed crossings / covered sensor-hour plus counter coverage, and shows the normalized rate, normalized monthly change and coverage in the month table. `royale.demand.traffic_summary` pools matched observed counts and positive coverage, and divides covered hours by scheduled *zone-sensor* hours rather than machine-hours. Missing count/coverage pairs contribute no rate exposure; scheduled exposure still includes all selected zone-days. Full-period mart totals: 30,214.95 covered / 30,401 scheduled zone-sensor hours, 99.3880% coverage and 87.8323 crossings/covered hour. Tests reconcile complete-period, single-DST-day and selected-zone states to independent mart sums; a synthetic unequal-coverage fixture distinguishes ratio of sums from mean daily rates and verifies zero/missing coverage behavior. Human approved the scope expansion in this session and on issue #86 before implementation.

**Machine readability.** The manager/adjusted/full ranking tables now identify total house-win dollars, expected/observed contribution per available hour ($/h), available hours, percent availability and hold, plus standardized house-win luck z. The page defines sign and units. Tests inspect rendered column metadata and compare every underlying ranking value with the unchanged shared summary.

**Remaining limits.** Observed counts remain valid counts, but coverage can confound temporal comparisons. Normalization cannot recover missed crossings or make missing hours representative.
